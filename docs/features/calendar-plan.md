# Calendar plan — Apple first

The owner's wishes for the app's calendar, written down on 29.10.2026 so the context isn't lost between sessions. **Approved so far:** Phase 1 (done) and Phase 2 (29.10.2026, step by step); Phase 3 waits for the owner's go. How the iCloud connection works: `docs/integrations/icloud-caldav.md`. What is built today: `docs/features/planning-calendar.md`.

## What the owner wants

- **Apple Calendar is the goal; Google is only a bridge.** "I don't use Google Calendar actually. I just need it to sync with Apple Calendar … our main focus should be on how Apple will handle it." Since 29.10.2026 the app talks to iCloud directly (CalDAV, an app-specific password), so Google may not be needed at all for this.
- **It must work as well as Google did.** "I have to be sure that iCloud Calendar would work as good as Google Calendar" — the reason for the Phase 1 test before anything is moved.
- **Nothing hard-coded** to the owner's account, calendars or a guess about Apple's answers: the app reads everything from iCloud (where the calendars live, their names, colours, events) and handles the standard in general.
- **Few phases.** "Parse it to phases (as less as possible)."
- **Past days are a record.** Nothing clears old tasks or days in bulk (`.claude/rules/tasks.md`); the Diary (Personal → Diary) is the day's log.

## What the app can read (29.10.2026)

The owner's Apple Calendar shows several accounts side by side; the iCloud connection reaches only one of them.

| On the iPhone | Where it really lives | Can the app read it? |
|---|---|---|
| The owner's own iCloud calendars | iCloud | **Yes** — Read my calendars read it (the first run, after the fix in #585) |
| Calendars subscribed to in iCloud (sport fixtures, name days, holidays) | A public feed (webcal / .ics) that iCloud only points to | **Not yet** — they are probably listed as "subscribed", not as calendars; their feed could be read from its address (server-side, through the SSRF guard) |
| A Google account's calendars, including ones shared with it | Google | **Not through iCloud** — only through the Google API (the app's Google connection, if it is that account) |
| A work account (Outlook / Exchange) | Microsoft | **Not through iCloud** — only through Microsoft Graph (an employer can block third-party apps), a published .ics link if work allows one, or a script on the iPhone (Scriptable reads every calendar the phone shows) |

This matters for Phase 3: "find a free time" is only as good as the calendars the app can see.

## Decisions (owner, 29.10.2026, "Go" for Phase 2)

- **Roles:** iCloud is the **only place the app writes** its events (Apple Calendar). Google is **read-only for calendars** — the Google account the app is connected to is the one that holds the shared family calendars. **Google Tasks stays exactly as it is** (the owner uses it).
- **Never the same event in both:** an iCloud copy first, confirmed, then the Google Calendar copy is removed (the item stays in the app).
- **Six calendars:** Training · Work (with Projects) · Personal (tasks, repeats) · Media (episodes, films) · Football · Games (owner, 31.10.2026: "Own").
- **No alert by default** (owner, 31.10.2026: "It should be off by default. But user should be able to activate it"): every area starts at None; the ready-made times and Custom… turn one on. (Until then: Training 60 min, Football 15, Work 10 — a setting saved earlier keeps its value.)
- **Emoji per area** in front of event titles (🏋️ Lower B, ⚽ a match, 📺 an episode).
- **Every choice is a setting** — "Add those to the settings as well, and other settings that you are coding but I couldn't change without asking you": each calendar's name, emoji, colour, default alert and whether it is used; which area an item goes to; what an event's notes carry. Values above are only the defaults.

## Phases

**Phase 1 — the Apple check (done, 29.10.2026).** Connect with an app-specific password; the app made a test calendar, wrote 9 test events, read them back and followed an event moved on the iPhone. Everything worked except Maybe and Private (no visible effect). Then **Read my calendars** (read-only): the owner's iCloud calendar reads; subscriptions, Google and work calendars don't (table above). Still to try: a deleted event through Check changes; whether iCloud splits repeating events into days.

**Phase 2 — the app's calendars in iCloud (approved 29.10.2026: "Go").** Items 19, 35, 24, 4, 3, 5, 6, 7 (12 by its fallback). Four steps, each its own PR, each checked by the owner before the next:

- **2a · Settings and the five calendars** (built 29.10.2026, migration 172). A **Calendar** tab in Settings: each area's name, emoji, colour, default alert and on/off (the defaults above). **Create in Apple Calendar** makes the area calendars in iCloud, or brings an existing one's name and colour up to date. The app-specific password is stored encrypted (AES-GCM, a key held as an Edge Function secret — one owner step). No event moves yet.
- **2b · Writing to iCloud.** *The original plan (29.10.2026; what was built is 2b-1 to 2b-3a below):* a setting "The app's calendar: Google / Apple" — Google until 2d. With Apple, every timed slot and repeating plan is written to its area's iCloud calendar by the server (an outbox like Google's `calendar_outbox`, drained every few minutes and right after a save): the title with the area's emoji, the area's alert, plain-text notes with a link back, the location. A repeating plan is one series, mapped exactly like Google's (`calendarSeries.ts`, mirrored into the function and checked). The sheet's chip says "Apple Calendar". Settings planned: which kind of item goes to which area (Games have no area of their own yet), what the notes carry.
  **2b design (30.10.2026).** Two PRs, so nothing changes for the owner until an area is switched:
  - **2b-1 — the writer, dormant** (built 30.10.2026). PR #588 (migration 173) was merged and deployed before its security review's fixes landed — still dormant, but storing event addresses in rows the browser and the assistant can write; migration 174 and the function update fix it (the warning in `AGENTS.md`). As fixed, migrations 173 + 174: `time_blocks` and `schedule_blocks` store only **which area calendar** an item's event is in (`icloud_area`) and iCloud's version of it (`icloud_etag`), a series also its start day and horizon — never an address: the function builds the address from its own server-only record of the area calendars and the item's id, so no browser or assistant write can steer a delete elsewhere (security review, 30.10.2026; the warning in `AGENTS.md`). `icloud_outbox` (service role only, owner lock, in `app_undo_outbox`); triggers on `time_blocks`, `schedule_blocks` and `tasks` queue a change only when an area is on Apple or the item already has an Apple event, and a change in Settings → Calendar re-queues the events already in that area. `icloud-calendar`'s `drain` (one at a time per account, a short lease) reads each queued item live; when it wants a calendar (its Google choice is Calendar or Both) and its area is on Apple, it PUTs the ONE event — the same rules as Google's events (`blockEventBody`, `seriesEventFor`, generated in by `scripts/sync-icloud-shared.mjs`) plus the area's emoji, alert and a link back — with If-Match, so a change made on the iPhone is never written over; otherwise it removes the event. The new copy first, then the old; a missing area calendar keeps the row waiting. Nothing was on Apple yet at 2b-1, so nothing was written.
  - **2b-2 — the switch** (built 30.10.2026, migration 175). Settings → Calendar per area: **Shows in: Google Calendar / Apple Calendar** (Apple once iCloud is connected; an area not in use stays on Google). Changing it opens a popup before anything moves: what each area moves ("12 events and 2 repeating plans" — to Apple *from today on* by default, or *every day*; past days stay in Google Calendar and move when changed), then **Move** saves, makes the calendars, moves and shows "Moving… 8 of 14", and says in one line where everything is (a warning when something stayed behind). The rule both ways is the owner's — the new copy first, confirmed, then the old one goes:
    - **To Apple:** `icloud-calendar`'s `move` queues the area's items that want a calendar (from today on: the days from today, plus earlier days of a repeat that have a slot of their own — they go with the series); the drain puts each in Apple Calendar, then hands its Google Calendar copy to Google's own delete queue (`calendar_outbox`) in one database step, `icloud_hand_over` — only an event id the app made from that item's own id. Any other id (a day moved in Google Calendar holds `<series>_…`; an id Google picked) is never deleted: its link is cleared, so its later fate in Google can't take the item off Apple Calendar (Google's read-back would), and it is counted (the warning in `AGENTS.md`). `google-tasks-sync` runs once at the end, so the Google copies go at once.
    - **A move that fails before anything moved** (a calendar not made, the queue not written, iCloud refusing the first send) puts the settings back as they were — an area is never left switched with nothing moved; a problem after that stops the run and is reported (the timer goes on). A first send that stopped part-way (iCloud's rate limit) keeps the switch when anything of those areas is already in Apple (`areaMoveApi.anyInApple`, cross-check 30.10.2026): the rest goes on the timer.
    - **Back to Google:** the browser makes each item's Google Calendar copy (a series keeps the start it had in Apple), and the drain removes the Apple copy of each one Google confirmed — an Apple copy stays until then (175: the Google copy appearing queues the item). One Google doesn't take stays in Apple Calendar and is counted.
    - **Afterwards:** an item in an Apple area never gets a Google Calendar event from the browser (`linkBlockCalendarEvent` and `linkScheduleSeries` ask `appleAreas.appleAreaOf` first); its save sends the queue at once (once more after 3 s when another run is sending) and confirms by the row's `icloud_area` (`itemCalendar.settleItemApple`) — only then does an old Google Task go; a copy not confirmed says so ("Saved · not in Apple Calendar yet (why) — tried again within minutes"). An Apple copy of an item moved to Google Tasks stays until its Google Task exists — except an all-day item whose slot the save removes (`app_save_item` drops a slot with no Google event): the save sends Google Tasks first, then Apple, so the order holds unless the 5-minute timer runs in those seconds. The sheet shows its choice even without a Google connection (Google Tasks still follows the real connection). iCloud can't be disconnected while an area shows in Apple Calendar. The sheet's chip says "Apple Calendar · busy" (Both: "Google Tasks + Apple Calendar"; none: "Only in this app"). A tick, a repeat's day and the app's opening send the queue too; a pg_cron timer every 5 minutes catches the rest (`ICLOUD_SYNC_SECRET`, an owner step). An older item (no Google choice stored) whose copy is in Apple reads as on Calendar (`presentGoogleTarget`, `repeat_day_presence`) — no data written for it.
    - The retired `icloud_href` / `icloud_outbox.href` columns are dropped (owner, 30.10.2026: "yes you can remove").
  - **2b-3a — notes and alerts** (built 30.10.2026, migration 177). Settings → Calendar → **Every Apple Calendar event** (Google Calendar's events are unchanged):
    - **Notes:** your notes + a link to the app (the default, as before), your notes only, the link only, or no notes. An event with nothing to say gets no notes field; the URL field always links to the app's day.
    - **All-day alert:** none (the default, as before), on the day or the day before, at a time (09:00 by default). Apple's all-day alert counts from the day's midnight, so 09:00 on the day is `PT540M` and 09:00 the day before `-PT900M`.
    - **"None" means none:** an area with alert "None" (and an all-day event with none) gets Apple's no-alert marker (`ACTION:NONE`, an alert of 01.04.1976 that never fires) — without any alert in the event the iPhone may add the owner's own default alert. *To confirm live* that the iPhone honours it.
    - A change to either setting re-sends every event already in Apple Calendar (177: `icloud_prefs_changed` queues every item with an Apple copy once), and the save says so ("events in Apple Calendar update in the background"). A re-send is not a change of the item: it never beats an edit made on the iPhone (the queue's `changed_at` stays as it was, and the read-back compares with that), and the drain sends the app's own changes first, the re-sends after them (25 a run). The values are compared as the app reads them, so the same value saved again, a value that reads as the default, or the first save after 177 (no such keys yet) sends nothing — 177 doesn't re-send everything by itself: an event already in Apple Calendar gets the no-alert marker the next time it is written (the owner's live test changes Football's alert, which re-sends those). A change to an area's emoji, alert, use or app re-sends that area's events the same way — as a re-send, never as a change of the item.
  - **2b-3b — decided 31.10.2026** (the owner's answers):
    - **Which item goes where:** no setting — the item's **Category** decides (the Add sheet shows it once an item has a time), with what the app knows of its origin (a Hevy routine → Training, an episode → Media, a project's task → Work): football → training → games → media → work → personal (`icloudEvents.itemArea`). The Add sheet's chip asks the same rule with the same inputs.
    - **Games** gets its own calendar (below, 2b-4).
    - **The read-back's timings** stay the server's (30 s on opening the app, 1 min after a read, the 5-minute timer) — no setting.
    - **What the iPhone keeps:** "Latest version wins" — the newer change wins, the app's or the iPhone's (as built in 2c: the app's later change rewrites the event). One case beyond the owner's words: a settings change that re-sends every event of an area (its name's emoji, notes, alerts) rewrites them too, so what was added only on the iPhone (an alert, a place) goes then as well.
    - **Time zones:** planned below (*Time zones from the phone*), waiting for the owner's go.
  - **2b-4 — everything to Apple** (built 31.10.2026, migrations 178, 179; owner: "Workouts and calendar items", "Do these and all other things to move to apple"):
    - **Games, its own calendar** "Lasci · Games" (🎮): every list of the app's area calendars takes it (178: the area checks, the read-back, the settings trigger). An item goes there by category Games or a game as its origin. Nothing moves until Games is switched to Apple in Settings → Calendar.
    - **Every Hevy workout as an event in "Lasci · Training"** (179), at its real start and end on Oslo's clock (an hour when Hevy gave no end), its Hevy notes per the notes setting, never an alert (it is done). Named by `hevy_workouts.icloud_key` — a uuid the database makes from Hevy's id — so nothing from outside is in an address. A workout already shown by its planned session's event (the ticked session) gets none of its own — never twice. Queued when it is added, deleted, or its title, notes or times really change (the Hevy syncs re-save workouts often: an unchanged one queues nothing), when a session's link to it changes, and when Training is switched; 179 queued every stored workout once where Training was already on Apple (the past trainings, (b)). Not read back from the iPhone (a record of what was done).
    - **No alert by default** in every area; the chip's calendar from the server's rule; the training move rule runs with `search_path ''` (178).
    - The switch popup's count ("12 events and 2 repeating plans") doesn't count workouts; they go with Training.
    - **What stays on Google after 2b-4** (honest list): an area until it is switched in Settings → Calendar (each area is its own switch; Games starts on Google); past days of an area moved *from today on* (they move when changed, or with *every day*); **Google Tasks** — an item without a time, or one set to Google Tasks, stays a Google Task (Apple Calendar holds events, not tasks); the **family calendars** and every other calendar the app shows are read from the Google account (read-only, 57); an event with invitees made on the iPhone is left to the iPhone.
- **2c · Changes from the iPhone** (built 30.10.2026, migration 176). Every send to Apple Calendar (`icloud-calendar`'s drain: a save, the app opening or coming back to the front — at most every 30 seconds — and the timer every 5 minutes while an area is on Apple) first reads what changed in each area calendar that can hold the app's events, inside the drain's lease, so nothing is written to iCloud meanwhile. The rules are Google's read-back's (`apply_calendar_readback`):
  - **Only the app's own events:** the file name the app makes, the whole address it builds itself (exact, never `startsWith`) and the uid inside. An event the owner made in an app calendar is left alone.
  - **What comes back:** a one-off **moved or renamed** → its slot and task follow; **deleted** → the item lets go of Apple Calendar (its calendar presence turns off; an all-day presence slot goes) and stays — never deleted or cancelled. A repeat **renamed** → the plan follows; **deleted** → the plan lets go of Apple Calendar; **one day moved** → that day becomes an item of its own (142) at its new day and time and gets its own event (the series leaves the day out); **one day deleted** → that day becomes its own item, off the calendar. Editing a repeat's rule or time on the iPhone ("all events", "all future events") is **not** followed — change a repeat's rule in the app (an "all future events" edit makes a second series the app doesn't know).
  - **Whose change wins:** an iPhone change is applied when it is someone else's — stamped `LAST-MODIFIED` by the iPhone (*to confirm live*), or a version the app didn't write (it knew its own) — unless a change of the app's own, made after it, waits to be sent: that one wins and goes out with iCloud's new version (the queue's `changed_at`, 177 — never `updated_at`, which other writes move, nor a re-send for a settings change, which is not a change of the item). The app's own write read back is never applied.
  - **What the iPhone keeps:** a change taken from the iPhone is not sent back, so the event stays as the owner left it there, with anything added on the iPhone (an alert, a place, notes). The next time the app changes that item (a tick, a rename in the app), its event is rewritten from the app and those additions go — keeping them (merging what only the iPhone has) is a decision for the owner. Notes, alerts, place and URL edited on the iPhone are not read back into the app. **An event with invitees** (added on the iPhone) is never rewritten or removed by the app — iCloud would email them in the owner's name; the event is read before every write, whatever the row stores; Settings → Subscriptions → Apple Calendar says how many events weren't sent and why, and the owner changes that one on the iPhone (cross-check, 30.10.2026). A symbol at the start of the owner's own title ("🎂 Birthday") stays when a change comes back; only the area's emoji (or one from before it changed) is taken off.
  - **An event deleted in iCloud while the app still holds its version** (a calendar made anew, removals held back) is made again on the app's next write, as Google's is (`If-None-Match`) — never refused for ever. A skipped day of a repeat comes off Apple Calendar, as it does off Google; Undo of the skip puts it back; a day marked missed stays.
  - **How quick:** the app's opening or coming back to the front (at most every 30 seconds) and the 5-minute timer read; a save reads too unless a read ran in the last minute. These numbers are constants for now (a setting in 2b-3b if the owner wants).
  - **The first read of a calendar** (no token yet) reads every event once, so a change made before 2c comes back too; a deletion is noticed only from a token on (a full read never infers one). The token (`icloud_sync_state`, RFC 6578) moves only when every change was handled; at most 120 events per run (the rest next run).
  - **A calendar renamed or recoloured on the iPhone** comes back into Settings → Calendar — only when it differs from what the app last wrote there (`written_name` / `written_color`), so a change made in the app, not yet written, is never undone. Read on the app's opening and the timer (a save skips it, for speed).
  - **Seen in the app:** Settings → Subscriptions → Apple Calendar says when changes were last read, and any problem (a calendar that can't be read, more than 20 removals held back, a change that couldn't be saved). An iPhone deletion of an item that still has its Google copy (kept until its replacement exists) only lets go of Apple — it stays on Google Calendar. Settings → Calendar saves only the fields changed, over the settings as they are now, so a name that came back from the iPhone is never put back.
  - **Limits (security review, 30.10.2026):** a repeat's days moved or deleted there count only from today on (a past day is a record — never an overdue task made for it), within the series' reach, at most 31 per change; more than 20 of the app's events removed from one calendar at once are not applied (the token stays and it is reported — a mass delete, a slip on Apple's side or a stolen session) — only events still linked to that calendar count, so the app's own deletes and moves never hold the read-back back; a one-off moved there lands only within a year of today (177); the read-back keeps to 25 s of a drain; a calendar's name and colour change only those two values in Settings (`icloud_calendar_look`); Undo of a delete puts an item back without its old Apple link, so the next read-back never takes it for deleted on the iPhone (`BLOCK_EVENT_RESET`, `SERIES_EVENT_RESET`).
  - What the read-back applies is not sent back (the iPhone has it). A rule the move sets off (a training session moved 7 or more days, 167/169) is the database's own decision: its writes are queued like a change made in the app and reach Apple (177 D, as they reach Google). iCloud's rate limit (503) stops a run; the timer tries again.
  - **Checked across features** (30.10.2026): `verify-db-icloud-cross` runs 171–177 on the live item, repeat, training, Google and Undo rules (a save, a tick, a delete and its Undo, a repeat's day, a training move read back from the iPhone, the hand-over, the settings re-send).
- **Time zones from the phone — a plan, waiting for the owner's go** (owner, 31.10.2026: "Time zones should be based on location … you can make a smart way to do it"):
  - **Today:** the app stores an item's time as a wall clock ("17:00") and writes every event in Oslo's time. "Gym 17:00" typed in Istanbul lands at 18:00 or 19:00 Istanbul time on the calendar (Istanbul is 1 hour ahead of Oslo in summer, 2 in winter).
  - **No location needed:** the phone already knows its time zone (it's what its clock uses — `Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone`).
  - **The plan:** each item saved keeps the phone's zone with its time (a `time_zone` column on slots; repeating plans already store one for Google, `google_calendar_time_zone`). Apple's and Google's events are written in that zone. An item whose zone isn't the phone's shows it small in the app ("17:00 Istanbul"), so a time never silently means two things. The read-back takes an iPhone move in the item's own zone.
  - **Size:** a migration, the item save, both calendar writers, the read-back and the day views' time labels — its own PR, after the owner's go.
- **2d · The move** — folded into 2b-2 (built there, per area, both ways). Google Tasks is untouched.
